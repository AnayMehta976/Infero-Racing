We didn’t form Inferno Racing just to enter a competition. We treated STEM Racing as a full engineering and enterprise challenge. Plenty of teams run on enthusiasm. We decided structure would be our advantage.

Grade 11 and 12 students representing The Shishukunj International School and the city of Indore, out to show younger students that STEM isn’t only something you study. It’s something you use.

The name

It started with The Flash. Not the red suit, but what sits underneath: controlled speed, backed by thinking. Inferno carries the same idea. Heat, energy, and power under control.

The IR-11

Designed in Fusion 360, tested in CFD, machined on a CNC from a single STEM Racing model block. Scroll to walk around it.

Three-quarter
14

  1. Body 22 g

    Shark-inspired: a narrow front, wide midsection and tapered rear. G1-continuous surfaces checked with zebra analysis.

  2. Nose cone 0.15 N

    High-nose idea from the 1992 Benetton B192, profile from a kingfisher’s beak. Design 3 of 3: lowest drag and fully within the rules.

  3. Front wing flat mainplane

    A simple, stable mainplane with endplates that keep lift-inducing vortices off the wheels.

  4. Halo & helmet 9.5 g

    Supplied parts bonded into a recess with no dimensional change, joined to the body with G1 continuity.

  5. Rear wing 3.64 g

    Teardrop support structure. The V2 wing was over-built, so V3 was thinned to a 1.48 safety factor against a 1.5 target.

  6. Wheels V4

    Four iterations cut moment of inertia from 691 to 337, a 51% drop, while staying rigid under a 50 N load.

Where the weight goes

55.7 g

From sketch to start line

Every decision is calculated and driven by data. Here’s how the IR-11 came together, one stage at a time.

01 Research

Understand the forces first

Before modelling anything we studied drag, the wheels’ moment of inertia, tipping moment and rolling resistance, and found the wake coming off the rotating wheels.

DragMoment of inertiaWheel wake
02 Inspiration

Borrowed from nature and F1

The body follows a shark: narrow front, wide middle, tapered rear. The nose takes the kingfisher’s beak profile and the high-nose idea from the 1992 Benetton B192.

BiomimicryTeardrop wing support
03 Car body

Smooth enough for the air to stay on

Zebra analysis showed where stripes broke at joints. We re-worked the nose-to-body and halo-to-body joins until the surfaces were G1 continuous.

G1 continuityFusion 360
04 Nose cone

Three designs, one winner

All three nose cones went through FEA and CFD. Design 3 uses one shorter vertical joint and a taller front wing, so air flows cleanly above and below it. It had the lowest drag and was the only one fully within regulation T8.7.

0.15 N dragSF 9.9T8.7 compliant
05 Rear wing

Strong enough, and no stronger

V1 came back with a safety factor of 7.8 and V2 with 18.2, far more material than the wing needed. For V3 we thinned the wing and its supports until FEA showed 1.48, right on our 1.5 target.

SF 18.2 → 1.483.64 g
06 Wheels

Lighter rims, faster starts

A heavy rim fights acceleration. Four wheel iterations reshaped the spokes and thinned the rim until moment of inertia dropped by half, while still passing a 50 N load test.

691 → 337 MOI−51%
07 CNC

Toolpaths before tools

Three setups (top, bottom and side milling) with a 3 mm ball-nose end mill, sized to the official model block. Fixing the bottom setup solved the sidepods our qualifier car couldn’t mill.

≈ 3 h 35 m toolpaths3 setups
08 Testing

Proven in CFD and in the lab

Full-car CFD at 22 m/s, streamlines staying attached over the body. Then a vernier calliper against the rulebook, a 200 g pull on the tether guides and a 3° incline roll test.

0.69 → 0.35 N200 g tether test

Lights out

In this class of racing, the result is the car plus your reaction. So we built our own start-light trainer to practise. Try it.

0.000 s
Press start, then tap or hit Space when the lights go out.
  • Five lights come on at uneven intervals, so you can’t count a rhythm.
  • The hold before lights out is random with an exponential spread up to 8 seconds. Guessing doesn’t pay.
  • Press too early and it’s logged as a jump start, just like a real false start.
